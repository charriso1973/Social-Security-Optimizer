**Established and correct (do NOT change):**
- AIME: top 35 indexed years / 420, floored to the dollar. PIA truncated to the dime. Benefits floored to the dollar.
- Early reduction 5/9% per month (first 36), 5/12% per month beyond; DRC 2/3% per month (8%/yr) for births 1943+, no DRCs past 70.
- Spousal excess = max(0, 50% of other's PIA − own PIA); spousal early reduction 25/36% per month (first 36), 5/12% beyond; no DRCs on spousal.
- Deemed filing: spousal excess is payable only once both have filed.
- Taxable SS formula and the $25k/$34k and $32k/$44k thresholds; Uniform Lifetime Table divisor 24.6 at 75.
- Under-60 indexing freezes at the latest published AWI year (SSA statement convention), paired with the bend points two years later. For 2027 bend points that means AWI 2025. This pairing is correct; keep it.
- The 81-pair grid, ranking by terminal cumulative value, and the reinvestment formula `cum = cum × (1 + nominal) + annual`.

**Audit findings this work order fixes (verified by running the engine 2026-11-14):**
1. FRA is hardcoded at 67 for every birth year (the input accepts 1920–2010).
2. The 2027 bend points are applied to every birth year, not the year the person turns 62. Born 1964, AIME $8,000 → engine $3,337.20, correct $3,263.20 (2026 bend points $1,286 / $7,749).
3. AWI data only goes back to 1987 and tax max to 1980. Earlier earnings are silently left unindexed (understates PIA), and pre-1980 earnings fall back to the 2026 cap (effectively uncapped).
4. The spousal reduction uses the recipient's own claim age, even when the spousal excess can't start until the other spouse files later. Test: A PIA $4,000 claims at 70, B PIA $1,000 claims at 62, same birth year → B at 70 gets **$1,350**; correct is **$1,700** ($700 own + $1,000 unreduced excess).
5. Survivor: no RIB-LIM floor (82.5% of deceased PIA), no survivor early reduction, no age-60 minimum, and if the deceased dies before claiming the survivor gets the deceased's *planned* benefit. Test: A PIA $4,000 claims at 62 and dies after age 63, B PIA $1,000 → B at 64 gets **$2,800**; correct is **$3,300**.
6. The solvency haircut applies to every year, not from the trust fund depletion year onward. The 83% default is from the 2024 Trustees Report.
7. "Future dollars" mode starts COLA in the year the older partner turns 62, but PIA is entered in today's dollars. Nominal values are understated for younger clients.
8. `simulateHousehold` computes `safeCola` but uses the raw `cola` for `inflationFactor` (engine.ts:195).
9. Tax snapshot: `currentYear = 2025` hardcoded (App.tsx:414). Standard deduction is the pre-OBBBA 2025 figure ($15,000/$30,000; current law $15,750/$31,500). The additional 65+ standard deduction is missing. No 37% bracket. No tax-exempt interest in provisional income. No QCD annual cap. If partners are different ages, the joint snapshot mixes balances from two different calendar years.
10. UI text is stale: the chart banner says "2026 SSA Actuarial Constants" (App.tsx:1122); the tax widget's "RMDs" row shows the after-QCD amount.

### Lane A — Per-person statutory core

### Lane B — Household rules
- [ ] **B1 Spousal timing.** Spousal excess for X starts in the first year both X and Y have filed: `spousalStartAge_X = max(claimAge_X, (birthYear_Y + claimAge_Y) − birthYear_X)`, i.e. X's age in the calendar year Y files, floored at X's own claim age. Base the spousal reduction on X's age at spousal start versus X's FRA (months). Pass case 8.
- [ ] **B2 Survivor.** When Y dies, survivor X receives `max(ownBenefit_X, survivorBenefit)`, computed as:
  - Base: if Y had claimed, Y's actual benefit (including DRCs), but if Y claimed before FRA the base is the PIA, subject to the RIB-LIM floor below. If Y died before claiming: before FRA → PIA; after FRA → PIA plus DRCs accrued to the age at death (max 70).
  - Survivor early reduction: if survivor benefits start before X's **survivor** FRA, reduce linearly to 71.5% at age 60 (28.5% spread over the months from 60 to survivor FRA). Survivor FRA table: born ≤1939 = 65; 1940 = 65y2m; 1941 = 65y4m; 1942 = 65y6m; 1943 = 65y8m; 1944 = 65y10m; 1945–1956 = 66; 1957 = 66y2m; 1958 = 66y4m; 1959 = 66y6m; 1960 = 66y8m; 1961 = 66y10m; 1962+ = 67 (verify against SSA's survivor FRA page and POMS RS 00615.301-series before coding).
  - RIB-LIM: if Y claimed before FRA, cap the survivor benefit at `max(Y's actual reduced benefit, 82.5% × PIA_Y)`.
  - Start: the later of the first year after Y's death and X's selected claim age (always ≥ 62, so the age-60 minimum is satisfied). This is a documented simplification; the optimizer doesn't separately search survivor-first/own-later sequencing.
  - Pass cases 9–11. Delete the "Simplification" / "Logic gap" comments that no longer apply.
- [ ] **B3 Solvency haircut start year.** Add `solvencyStartYear` (engine parameter + UI field shown when the haircut is on). Apply the factor only for `year >= solvencyStartYear`. Look up the **latest** Trustees Report (2026 if released; else 2025: OASI depletion 2033 / 77% payable, OASDI 2034 / 81%). Put both sets in `constants.ts` with the report year and URL. Default to OASI, since retirement and survivor benefits are paid from the OASI fund. Pass case 15.
